  1. Raw2 processor formats · 158K txns
  2. Bronze → Silver6 quality rules · 175/175 errors quarantined

// Limitations I'd flag to a stakeholder
  • Transactions are synthetic, generated with realistic fraud patterns, so the £ figures show the method rather than a real bank's numbers.
  • The A/B test (≈1,870 customers per arm) could only detect effects of £0.85 or more. The observed £4.64 effect is well above that, but smaller effects would need a longer test.
  • The model raises false alarms slightly (+0.05 per customer per 30 days). That is inside the pre-agreed guardrail, but worth monitoring after launch.

Case study: Fraud Risk Lakehouse

Problem

Rule-based fraud alerts missed most fraud and wasted analyst time. The question: would a model cut total cost, not just raise accuracy?

Data

158K synthetic card transactions from two processor formats, with 175 errors injected on purpose. A PySpark Bronze/Silver/Gold pipeline with 6 data-quality rules quarantined 100% of them, with de-duplication and PII masking before any modelling.

Model

XGBoost on leak-free window features with a time-based split, and a decision threshold chosen in pounds. PR-AUC 0.80, ROC-AUC 0.98, recall up from 21% to 80%.

Experiment

Customer-randomised A/B test with power analysis, a sample-ratio-mismatch check and a false-alarm guardrail: £4.64 saved per customer (95% CI £2.33–£7.26, p < 0.001).

Impact

Alert cost down 73% (£24.1K → £6.5K), a projected £351K a year. SHAP reasons on every alert, a fairness check, MLflow tracking and 31 automated tests in CI.
